facts interviewFlags.inconsistencies.earnedIncomeWithoutJob
Earned income with no job behind it can't be verified or updated when the job changes, and is often a job that ended but wasn't closed out.
facts interviewFlags.inconsistencies.householdSizeDiffersFromMembers
The household's size decides its benefit. A size that doesn't match the members listed means someone is missing or counted who shouldn't be.
facts interviewFlags.inconsistencies.incomeBelowExpenses
When reported income doesn't cover reported expenses, something the application doesn't show is paying the difference: unreported income, or help from outside the household. FNS-1104 makes it a probe.
facts interviewFlags.inconsistencies.incomeDiffersFromPayStub
A recorded amount that doesn't match its pay stubs usually means a pay frequency was converted wrongly — weekly, every two weeks, and twice a month are easy to confuse.
facts interviewFlags.inconsistencies.jobWithoutEarnedIncome
A job with no income recorded is information that arrived but wasn't applied — the most common kind of agency error.
facts interviewFlags.inconsistencies.utilitiesIncludedButBilled
A household that pays a utility bill isn't one whose utilities are all included in the rent, and which it is decides whether a utility allowance applies. What counts as a utility deduction is a common misapplication of policy.
facts interviewFlags.inconsistencies.wagesDifferFromWageRecords
Income an agency's data systems show but nobody confirms is an error the agency could have caught. A difference from the wage records needs explaining before it's resolved either way.
facts interviewFlags.risks.cashOrSelfEmploymentIncome
Cash, gig and self-employment income often isn't on the application at all, and isn't asked about unless someone asks specifically.
facts interviewFlags.risks.childrenAndEarnersWithoutDependentCare
Care for a child so a household member can work is deductible. When it's new it often isn't asked about, which leads to an underpayment.
facts interviewFlags.risks.earnedIncomeVaries
Earned income is the largest source of payment errors, and wages that change month to month are the hardest to get right: a single pay stub doesn't show what a month of irregular hours or tips comes to.
facts interviewFlags.risks.elderlyOrDisabledWithoutMedicalCosts
A member 60 or older, or disabled, may deduct medical costs. Missed deductions are among the most common fixable errors, and lead to underpayments.
facts interviewFlags.risks.householdChangedSincePrevious
Who lives in the household and buys food together decides whose income counts. Changes are often unreported, and often not asked about at recertification.
facts interviewFlags.risks.incomeChangedSincePrevious
Income that's changed since the household on record — a job gained or lost, a raise, hours cut — is a leading cause of both overpayments and underpayments when it isn't reported. On a new application, that's since the application was filed.
facts interviewFlags.risks.multipleProgramsApplied
Households on several programs face reporting rules that differ between them, which makes it harder to keep each program's information right.
facts interviewFlags.risks.shelterCostsCarriedOver
Shelter costs carried forward from the last certification without being asked about can be out of date in either direction: a move or a rent increase that's missed.
